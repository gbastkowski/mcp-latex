# The Daily Renderer

## Typesetter Declares Victory Over Rogue En-Dash

### Ligature Suppressed After Weeks Of Complaint; Compositors Admit Fault

*By our own correspondent — at the composing stone*

![](../img/fig-portrait.pdf){width=42%}

A long-running dispute between a serif face and a pair of hyphens ended
yesterday in what observers described as a decisive settlement. For weeks,
readers of these pages had complained that every `--pdf-engine` in the technical
columns arrived at their breakfast tables as `–pdf-engine`, a single melancholy
dash where two upright ones belonged.

The cause, once identified, proved mundane. Palatino, a font of otherwise
impeccable manners, had been applying its common ligatures inside verbatim
passages — a courtesy welcome in prose and disastrous in code. Compositors have
since instructed the monospace family to abandon the practice entirely.

"Copy the text out of the paper now and it is the text you wanted," said a
spokesman for the composing room, declining to elaborate on how long the
condition had gone unnoticed.

The settlement is not expected to affect prose, where the en-dash retains all
its former privileges — as this sentence demonstrates.

## Latin Modern Sans Quietly Dismissed After Years Of Service

### Appointment Was Never Formally Made, Records Show

The sans-serif face that had, without invitation, been setting every heading in
these pages was relieved of its duties this week. Its appointment was never
formally made: asked for a sans and given no instruction, the typesetting engine
had simply reached for the nearest one to hand.

Readers had noticed. The face sat too light against the body text, its
proportions belonging to a different century than the copy it introduced.

Optima now takes the headings in the corporate edition, on the reasoning that
Hermann Zapf drew both it and the body face of that edition, and that two hands
from the same workshop tend to agree.

## Home News

### A Note On The Rules

The heavy rule that formerly ran beneath the running head on every page has been
reduced to a hairline. The thick strokes now appear only in the nameplate above,
where they belong. Correspondence on the matter is closed.

### Figures Return To Their Proper Height

![](../img/fig-dial.pdf){width=52%}

This edition sets its numerals as old-style figures, so that 1846 and 118.2 sit
within the line rather than standing above it in the manner of a ledger. Readers
of a traditional disposition are expected to approve.

## Correspondence

> Sir — Your columns of last week set a table inside a two-column measure and
> the result was a catastrophe of overlapping rules. I trust the responsible
> party has been spoken to.
>
> — A Reader, Hamburg

The party has been spoken to. The two-column measure has been withdrawn
entirely; tables and code now enjoy the full width of the page, and this
newspaper's character is carried by its nameplate and its typography rather than
by narrow columns it could not properly support.

> Sir — Why does this newspaper print no table of contents?
>
> — Another Reader, Bremen

Because it is a newspaper.
